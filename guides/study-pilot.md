# A six-week study pilot

Use six weeks of **your actual study** to improve the plan. Start at your current demonstrated skill, including after a break. This is a small personal planning exercise, not a controlled study or an extra course. No learner results have been collected for this template.

<a id="start"></a>
## Set up once

1. Copy the [pilot log](../resources/templates/STUDY-PILOT.md) into a private notes file or `study-private/` in your local checkout. That folder is ignored by Git. A selectively shared copy is optional; remove personal details before sharing.
2. Record your current [semester milestone](first-semester.md#semester-plan), a realistic weekly time budget and known exams or other commitments. Start with the plan's assumptions only if they fit your life.
3. Choose **one main outcome** for the next week and its artifact/readiness check. Practice, project work and review all fit inside the same budget.
4. Bookmark the exact lesson/task and put that link in the [resume note](progress-tracker.md#resume). Do not copy a second schedule into the log.

<a id="session"></a>
## After a study session: about two minutes

Record the task you intended, approximate focused minutes, the artifact or output, one piece of readiness evidence and the next action. Separately note setup/access time when it explains the difference. Do not reconstruct forgotten times as precise measurements; write “unknown” when needed.

Choose the obstacle that best describes what happened; multiple causes are possible:

- **Access:** lecture/login/download/network/tool installation prevented work. Record the exact failure and try the listed local or public fallback.
- **Instructions:** you could not identify the next step, expected output or meaning of “done.” Note the page/heading and a proposed clearer instruction.
- **Knowledge:** a specific prerequisite or concept blocked the task. Choose the corresponding [readiness recovery](readiness-checks.md#how-to-check).
- **Life/time:** exams, health, family, fatigue or a changed schedule reduced capacity. No explanation of private circumstances is required; reduce or pause planned work.
- **Scope:** the required task was larger than the available budget. Finish a smaller meaningful stage and carry the remainder forward explicitly.
- **None / uncertain:** progress was clear, or you cannot yet identify a cause. Avoid diagnosing ability from one difficult session.

<a id="weekly-review"></a>
## At the end of each week: about ten minutes

Open the artifact and repeat one short check without the solution. Record assistance used. Use a changed input or explanation to distinguish remembering the supplied example from being able to apply it.

Then compare planned work with **finished evidence**, not just videos watched. Record one confusing instruction or gap, one thing to retain and **one change** for next week. If there was no study, record that without inventing results. Restart from the last demonstrated skill using the [recovery path](first-semester.md#recovery).

Weeks 1–6 of this pilot mean six calendar review intervals beginning when you start the log. They need not match semester weeks 1–6. Pauses and incomplete tasks are useful observations; the pilot has no pass score.

<a id="six-week-review"></a>
## After six weeks: change the plan using evidence

Use the six weekly notes to answer:

1. **Pacing:** how much time was actually sustainable in ordinary weeks versus exam/illness weeks? Budget the next milestone from comparable weeks; reduce scope or extend the calendar when needed.
2. **Learning:** which artifacts can you reproduce, explain and adapt independently? Keep demonstrated work complete. Identify one specific remediation task for anything still partial.
3. **Resources:** did a repeated access problem or a mismatch in explanations justify changing the default? Try a targeted alternative on the same small task before replacing an entire course.
4. **Project scope:** which stage yielded useful practice, and which added tooling without helping the learning objective? Defer the latter while keeping the assessed outcome intact.
5. **Navigation:** where did you lose your place or misread optional work as required? Record the exact source page and proposed improvement. A private note is sufficient; reporting it is optional.
6. **Next decision:** keep the route, change one thing, or pause with a concrete resume note. Set a review date and a condition that would make you reconsider.

Write a short decision memo in the template: observations → interpretation → change → how to check whether it helped. Six weeks from one learner cannot establish a universal completion time or that a teaching method caused improvement. Useful personal feedback still warrants a practical adjustment.

<a id="design-evidence"></a>
## Why this structure

Short recall checks and revisiting skills across sessions are informed by research on practice testing and distributed practice. Staged examples can help early programming performance, but successful guided work does not prove later independent transfer. The [research record](../audit/learning-experience-research-2026-11-05.md) states the sources and their limits.

The six-week duration, small log and obstacle categories are **design choices to make revision manageable**, not scientifically optimal intervals. Repository walkthroughs by agents are proxy inspections; they are separate from your future observations.
