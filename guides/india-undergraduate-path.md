# First-year CSE/AI in India: build international options

This route is for a BTech CSE/AI student who has not chosen a job family and wants to work at a strong company in India or abroad. **Build the common foundation now; choose a provisional specialization after trying the work.** A degree label, a fashionable framework or finishing every module is not an employment guarantee.

The [employer research](../audit/career-research-2026-11-05.md) inspected 12 postings and official student-program guides in November 2026. It informs the exercises below; it cannot predict vacancies, visa policy or the best-paid role in your graduation year. Lectures remain free, paid books are welcome, and buying a book or cloud subscription is not an entry requirement.

<a id="first-year-priorities"></a>
## What to prioritize this year

| Priority | Work to do | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| College foundations | Follow your degree's programming, discrete mathematics, calculus and linear algebra courses; use M0/M2/M3 to repair specific gaps | Solve a fresh problem and explain the method; record which roadmap outcomes college work already covers |
| Programming fluency | Complete M1 in stages: [Week 0](week-0.md), [first 30 days](first-30-days.md), starter project, then tested Weather CLI | Write, debug and test code you can explain without an assistant; handle malformed input and failed requests |
| Algorithms and software habits | Learn M4 after functions and data structures are comfortable; introduce Git, debugging, tests and readable functions as you build | Explain complexity, solve a changed problem, review a diff and correct a failing test |
| Data literacy | Begin M7 and M8a after M1; use one small permitted dataset for both | Reconcile totals, explain joins/missing values and write a short answer to a real question |
| Communication and collaboration | Explain one technical result to a classmate; work on a small shared project or request a specific review | A clear README, review response, recorded decision and honest account of your contribution |

Your college's C++/Java programming course can develop the same fundamentals; keep Python for this roadmap's exercises without collecting multiple languages at once. Learn OS, networks, DBMS and software engineering when they arrive in your degree, then demonstrate the relevant project behavior. An AI specialization does not replace general CSE competence.

Protect college study and the eligibility it supports. Record actual campus/employer grade, backlog and graduation rules when relevant; this roadmap invents no universal CGPA cutoff. Use a sustainable weekly budget and reduce optional work during exams. The [four-year schedule](four-year-plan.md#weekly-template) is an example, not additional work on top of every college assignment.

**Your next four weeks:** follow the [first-30-days plan](first-30-days.md), or its demonstrated-skill equivalent if you already know Python. Keep a one-line learning log and choose one small project. Role selection, public hosting, advanced agents and overseas applications do not gate this first step.

<a id="role-experiments"></a>
## Explore the work before choosing the title

Spread these bounded experiments across the first two years as prerequisites become available. They are learning samples, not three simultaneous courses or proof of full module completion. Reuse a domain such as transport, campus operations or public energy data, with permission and sanitized fixtures where needed.

| Experiment | When ready | Deliverable and what it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| Analysis and business decisions | After M1/M7; add M8a SQL when ready | Answer a question, reconcile spreadsheet/SQL totals, define a KPI and show a filtered chart plus decision memo. Did you enjoy investigating messy data and explaining a decision? |
| Software and AI applications | Start with the M1 CLI; add M18-app only after its HTTP/validation/test prerequisites | Build a useful deterministic tool first. Later compare a small model on frozen examples, expose a simple interface and handle failures. Did you enjoy debugging, integration and user behavior? |
| Statistical modeling | After the relevant M2/M3/M5 and M9/M10 foundations | Predict a defined outcome with a baseline, defensible split, train-only preprocessing and error analysis. Did you enjoy mathematical reasoning, evaluation and revising hypotheses? |

If data pipelines are the engaging part, explore the [data-engineering route](practitioner-track.md#role-pathways). If reading/replicating papers is more engaging, explore the research route after the relevant theory. Software engineering is also a plausible first destination: use your college CS work and software projects alongside this roadmap instead of forcing an AI job title.

After each experiment, record what you liked, what you could demonstrate, what feedback exposed, and what you would investigate next. By the end of the second year, choose **one provisional primary route** and a related alternative. Revisit the choice when new work or evidence changes it.

<a id="career-evidence"></a>
## Build evidence matched to the role

| Direction | Portfolio evidence to strengthen | A mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Analyst / product analytics | SQL, spreadsheet reconciliation, metric definitions, a usable dashboard and a concise decision memo | Showing attractive charts without checking grain, totals or denominators |
| Applied data science | Statistical design, predictive baselines, leakage controls, uncertainty and error slices | Calling a BI-only project ML experience, or presenting accuracy without an appropriate baseline |
| ML / data engineering | Reusable code, data/model contracts, incremental processing, monitoring and a reproduced failure/recovery | Treating cloud service names or deployment screenshots as evidence of correctness |
| AI application engineering | A small complete application, typed API boundaries, real-model evaluation, permissions and failure handling | A chat interface with no measured task quality or tests |
| Research foundations | Relevant theory, careful paper replication, related-work comparison, ablations and limitations | Assuming course certificates replace the scientific evidence or qualifications a research role requests |

For analyst practice, use the [M7 reconciliation extension](../curriculum/2-statistics-and-data.md#analyst-reconciliation). A free spreadsheet such as [LibreOffice Calc](https://www.libreoffice.org/discover/calc/) supports the concepts. An employer requesting Excel/Power BI may still assess those products; do not claim product-specific experience you did not acquire. [Microsoft Learn's Power BI paths](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/powerplatform/power-bi) are self-paced training, distinct from paid instructor-led classes/certification and product sharing licenses. A local workbook/report and sanitized screenshots are sufficient roadmap artifacts.

For application roles, build the [M18-app integration extension](../curriculum/6-frontier-production.md#application-integration) when you can already explain the model boundary. One plain HTML interface or a simple Python UI is enough; a specific frontend framework is not a universal requirement.

<a id="international-options"></a>
## Separate the three international goals

| Route | What to check in the actual opening | Planning implication |
|---|---|---|
| Global company, role based in India | Office location, campus/off-campus eligibility, graduation window, accepted project/internship experience and work arrangement | A useful destination in its own right; it can develop experience but does not promise a later overseas transfer |
| Role physically based abroad | Location, work authorization, role-specific sponsorship, degree/graduation conditions and relocation requirements | A US/Canadian company name or a general sponsorship FAQ does not establish your eligibility for that job |
| Remote work from India | Countries/states from which work is permitted, employment/contract arrangement and working hours | “Remote US” is not automatically “work from any country”; resolve unstated eligibility before relying on the opportunity |

Compare the work, mentorship, engineering practice, growth, compensation relative to living costs and employment terms alongside company reputation. Further study abroad is a later, separate decision about academic goals, funding, total cost and current work rights; it is not a required paid step in this roadmap.

<a id="student-opportunities"></a>
## Look for programs at the right student stage

| Official source | Observed program scope | Action |
|---|---|---|
| [Microsoft Explore](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/exploremicrosoft) | Dedicated page describes first-/second-year students; an 8-week India program and a 12-week US program | Monitor the India openings and their exact eligibility. The program page is not proof that applications are open today or that you are eligible for the US version |
| [Amazon student internships](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/career-programs/university/internships-for-students) | Official student opportunity directory | Read each opening's degree stage, graduation window, location and dates rather than assuming one company-wide rule |
| [Atlassian early careers](https://www.atlassian.com/company/careers/earlycareers) | Internships target penultimate-year students; graduate programs target final-year/recent graduates | Record current recruitment windows when your cohort is eligible; recheck future cycles |
| College faculty, clubs and permitted community projects | Scope, supervision and availability depend on the specific opportunity | Agree a small deliverable, schedule, data rights and feedback process; retain evidence of your contribution |

Check internship duration, weekly hours, pay and travel before committing. A six-month weekday internship may conflict with a full-time semester even if its listing welcomes students. A paid certificate, referral, unpaid role or accepted open-source PR is not a mandatory gate. Do not send applications for experienced roles merely because their title includes “I” or “junior.”

<a id="semester-review"></a>
## Refresh the plan each semester

1. **Review your evidence:** reproduce one project, solve one fresh problem and explain one failure/trade-off. Get feedback where possible.
2. **Inspect a small balanced sample:** read around 10–15 direct employer postings for your two plausible routes and eligible geography/seniority. Include ordinary employers as well as recognizable names. Use the [opportunity template](../resources/templates/OPPORTUNITIES.md); a sample is not a labor-market estimate.
3. **Separate gates from preferences:** record required, preferred and unstated degree, experience, graduation, location and work-authorization conditions. Count academic projects only where allowed.
4. **Choose one change:** close a repeatedly relevant skill gap, improve a weak project or change a specialization. A new framework name alone is insufficient reason to restart.
5. **Plan applications when eligible:** update the resume and rehearse the relevant [interview tasks](career-operations.md#interview-practice). Record known openings/deadlines, not predicted dates years ahead.

Keep the mathematical, CS and communication foundation stable while updating implementation choices. Avoid a weekly roadmap rewrite driven by headlines; actual work, eligibility and feedback are better reasons to change course.
