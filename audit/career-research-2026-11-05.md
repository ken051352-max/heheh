# Employer-posting research for roadmap career fit

Inspected 2026-11-05, approximately 06:50-07:10 UTC, using live Exa search and full-page fetch. This is a deliberately selected, small qualitative sample of 12 readable employer career/ATS postings: eight India roles and four Canada/US comparisons. It is not a prevalence study, a salary survey, a ranking, or a list of guaranteed available vacancies. No applications or messages were sent.

## Access and sampling method

Searches sought analyst, data scientist, data engineer, ML engineer and AI-application titles with graduate/early-career language. Aggregators were used only to discover leads; the entries below were read at the employer's career domain or its named ATS board. Posting bodies were readable and contained no closure notice in the returned text. That does not verify application submission availability or recruiter headcount. Reopen the official page before applying. Many employers omit a publication date; an inspection date must not be represented as the posting date.

Exact wording under a required/basic heading is separated from preferred qualifications. A degree alternative or project allowance is recorded only when present. No assumption is made that a portfolio waives a stated degree, work authorization, location, or experience requirement. Work authorization and sponsorship were not established for these roles; country labels are not permission to work there. No explicit security-clearance requirement was established in these extracts, which is not proof that none could apply in later screening.

## Curriculum implications: editorial synthesis, not employer prevalence

1. **Make analyst spreadsheet/BI competence concrete.** Existing M7 names Excel-to-Python and BI tools but a tool mention is not an assessed outcome. Add an analyst-specific artifact: clean a workbook, reconcile totals to SQL, use lookups/pivots with explicit missing-data rules, define KPI grain/denominators, build a dashboard with filters and a small metric dictionary, and write a decision memo. Preserve a free local route; do not require paid SaaS publication. Evidence: J01/J02; TE J03 uses visualization for DS too.
2. **Distinguish role evidence.** Analyst dashboard/report evidence is valuable for J01/J02 but Arcadia J04 explicitly wants model-building and evaluation. A DS candidate should show a defensible split, baseline, error slices, uncertainty and integration; an AI-app candidate should show a reliable API/tool workflow; a DE candidate should show correct data contracts and replay. Do not require every applicant to finish all three tracks.
3. **Add a small end-to-end software slice for AI applications.** J06/J07/J09/J10 make APIs, debugging/tests, data models and user-facing integration concrete. One accessible interface plus a typed backend, persistence, error/loading states and integration tests is stronger role evidence than framework name collection. HTML/CSS/JS basics can be a targeted extension; React is not universally required by this sample.
4. **Assess operational work at realistic scale.** J04/J05/J11 require reusable code, monitoring, data quality and documented decisions. Require one reproduced failure, meaningful regression test and rollback/replay explanation; large cloud spend and Kubernetes are not universal entry requirements.
5. **Use an eligibility checklist before skill matching.** Record required location/work arrangement, degree/discipline and alternatives, graduation window, years and accepted experience types, work authorization/sponsorship, notice-period/availability, and internship duration/pay. Distinguish requirement, preference and unstated. J02/J05/J11 show why I/junior/new-grad labels do not replace this check. J07 shows why preferences should not be hardened into automatic rejection.
6. **Build role-specific interview rehearsals as learner exercises.** Analyst: live SQL, spreadsheet reconciliation, explain a misleading chart and metric definition. DS: split/leakage diagnosis, metric/threshold choice, experimental design and error-analysis narrative. DE: incremental pipeline/replay/schema-change failure. MLE: debug train-serving mismatch and discuss resource tradeoffs. AI-app: trace a failed tool workflow, validate schemas and demonstrate approval/idempotency. These are proposed practice rubrics based on job tasks, not verified interview question frequencies.
7. **Treat domain depth as an elective specialization.** J08 and J12 require vision/robotics context, data capture and hardware constraints. Do not add ROS/C++/camera calibration as a universal data-science completion gate. For research-oriented roles, clarify degree/scientific-evidence expectations and that course completion alone is not a research credential.
8. **Make collaboration demonstrable.** Add a concise design note, code review response, stakeholder explanation and failure/postmortem narrative to the portfolio. Personal/public-data projects are valid; don't invent users, business revenue or employment experience.

### Recommended adaptation of the roadmap

- **Keep a common foundation while exploring.** First-year priorities: college CS/math coursework, Python fluency, DSA/problem solving, Git/testing, basic SQL and clear explanations. Preserve later college OS, networks, DBMS and software-engineering depth; an AI-specialization syllabus is not a substitute for general CSE foundations. Do not add every production tool to first-year study.
- **Try small role samples before choosing.** After the relevant basics, complete one bounded analytics question, one tiny evaluated ML model and one useful software/AI application; reuse a domain/dataset where appropriate. Reflect on which work was engaging and which gaps matter. Choose a provisional primary route only after evidence, and revisit it each semester. These are exploration artifacts, not claims of full module mastery.
- **Maintain college performance without an invented universal cutoff.** Record the actual campus/company/research-program grade and backlog rules when applying. Protect time for degree coursework; do not claim a fixed CGPA guarantees hiring or substitute public GitHub activity for formal eligibility.
- **Separate three geographic goals.** (1) Global-company roles located in India; (2) roles physically based abroad with explicit work-authorization/sponsorship terms; (3) remote roles whose permitted work countries must be checked. Company headquarters and brand are not job location. An India role can provide valuable experience but does not guarantee an overseas transfer. Further study abroad is a separate later decision about research goals, admission, funding, total cost and work rights, not a mandatory paid step in this free-lecture roadmap.
- **Use stage-appropriate internships.** First-/second-year programs like Explore and small campus/faculty/team projects can fit earlier stages; penultimate-year internships like Atlassian's require waiting for the eligible cohort. Read duration, travel and weekday-work demands: Twimbit's six-month 9-6 internship may conflict with a full-time semester despite its broad student eligibility.
- **Refresh evidence each semester and before application season.** Read a small balanced set of direct employer postings for the two most plausible routes and desired countries. Record date, location, required/preferred/unstated gates, accepted experience, tasks and portfolio evidence. Change a tool or project when the evidence warrants it; keep foundations stable. Do not convert a handful of postings into market shares, a salary forecast or a prediction of the best role in 2030.
- **Judge progress by transferable evidence.** Solving a fresh problem, explaining tradeoffs, writing tests, debugging a failure, collaborating and documenting a defensible decision survive individual-tool changes. Introduce modern AI-assisted development together with independently verifying generated code; don't let generation replace skill assessment.

## Limits

This sample is intentionally skewed toward readable English-language public postings and selected role titles. It misses campus hiring, private recruiter pipelines and sites blocked to extraction. A readable older posting may remain indexed after hiring stops. Job descriptions may contain errors, demanding wish lists or inconsistent qualifications; no acceptance rates, salary medians or universal hiring timelines can be inferred. Employer statements were recorded as statements, not endorsed as fair screening rules or evidence that all candidates must possess them. All dates and requirements need refreshing before an actual application.
